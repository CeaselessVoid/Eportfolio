Ice Buoy Frame Design

This project involved the design of a housing structure for a power subsystem, sensing rig, and processing unit that could be deployed on sea ice to measure wave interaction in the Marginal Ice Zone in the Antarctic.

The frame was designed such that the system would remain buoyant should the surrounding ice melt, allowing it to continue operation while protecting the internal components. The components would be held in a separate rectangular housing for added protect from water ingress.

The internal frame would feature insulating foam to protect equipment from harsh cold as well as aid in the buoyancy of the design. This feature was not implemented in the prototype design. The prototype was 3D printed while the internal housing was laser cut. A practical model would use an aluminum alloy to be more robust to harsh weather.

Key Features

  • Threaded lid and O-ring: Provides a watertight seal when the housing is closed.
  • Grooved lid: Allows easier sealing of the housing.
  • Tapered base: Facilitates water runoff from the structure.
  • Rounded sides: Improves the dynamic response of the structure to waves.
